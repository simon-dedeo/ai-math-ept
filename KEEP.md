# What to keep

Rough handoff note:

- Keep the central claim: the important variable is the **amortization horizon**, not an essential human/AI proof style. Proofs simultaneously serve a kernel, the current construction episode, and future users.
- Keep the strongest evidence: 3,630 exact-statement pairs; the lexical consolidation crossover (AI supplies more claims, humans more claims referenced at least twice); human equal-node construction graphs are denser and more branching; AI source boundaries disappear more often but retained binders fan out more in elaborated terms; humans state callable families about three times as often.
- Keep the conceptual contributions: consumer-invariance, the consolidation--composition split, counterfactual width, and library-amortized proof complexity over theorem streams.
- Keep the negative results and limits: provenance is not causal; conditional claim duration is unresolved; absolute multi-consumer-site supply is denominator-dependent; names are not proven semantically better; certificate topology is representation-specific; surprisal is exploratory; global library reuse is unobserved.
- Keep `report/horizon/main.tex`, the final PDF, analysis/extraction code and tests, and the committed machine-readable summaries/hashes needed to audit the paper.
- Delete only transient products that are cheaply regenerated: LaTeX auxiliaries, test caches, bytecode, and temporary page renders. Do not discard the exact evidence bundle merely because the pipeline can eventually recompute it.

Next serious test: randomize the same agents between compile-first and persistent-library objectives on fixed theorem streams, then measure later proof cost, repair, retrieval, API survival, and human comprehension.
