\large Same Theorem, Different Interior}
\author{Working paper}

\begin{abstract}
Two correct proofs of the same theorem can be built very differently.  We ask whether those
architectural differences also change the epistemic support that a proof network provides.  The
question extends Viteri and DeDeo's account of epistemic phase transitions, in which converging
deductive and abductive pathways allow confidence to survive local fallibility.

We compare \SourcePairs{} theorem-matched human- and AI-provenance Lean scripts and, in a separately
filtered sample, \TermPairs{} matched elaborated proof-term networks.  The theorem strongly constrains
both populations: human and AI proof sizes correlate at $\rho_s=\LineRho$ in source and
$\rho_s=\NodeRho$ after elaboration.  Within that common frame, AI proofs have a larger interior.
They contain \HaveRatio{} times as many local claims per tactic and \InteriorRatio{} times as many
non-constant term nodes per distinct library constant.  Their named interface changes much less.
Yet the Viteri--DeDeo model assigns nearly the same theorem belief to both populations, and interior
expansion has little association with belief change ($\rho_s=\DecouplingRho$).

We call this pattern \emph{epistemic decoupling}: proof architecture can change without a
commensurate change in modeled epistemic outcome.  The result is not a claim that all proofs are
equally understandable.  It identifies the missing scientific object: the interface through which
a checked certificate becomes available to a reader and a mathematical community.  For AI-for-math,
the next problem is therefore not only proof search, but proof design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two proofs can agree on the theorem and disagree on nearly everything else.  They can choose
different lemmas, introduce different intermediate claims, and distribute their work across
different levels of abstraction.  In informal mathematics these differences are familiar.  In a
proof assistant they become measurable, because both constructions can be checked against exactly
the same formal statement.

This creates a new problem for the study of mathematical reasoning.  When correctness is held fixed,
which features of a proof remain epistemically important?  The question is practical as well as
theoretical.  AI systems can now produce large numbers of kernel-checked proofs
\citep{yang2023,ren2025,hubert2026}.  Correctness is indispensable, but abundance makes selection
unavoidable.  We must decide which certificates are worth reading, maintaining, and reusing.

We propose a simple answer and give it a name.  \emph{Epistemic decoupling} occurs when alternative
proofs of the same theorem differ substantially in architecture but not in an epistemic outcome.
In the present study, architecture is the amount of internal derivational structure placed behind
a proof's named contact with the library.  The epistemic outcome is theorem belief under the network
model of \citet{viteri2022}.  The term is deliberately narrow.  It describes a relation between a
structural contrast and a specified epistemic functional; it does not declare proof structure
irrelevant in general.

The paired human--AI comparison reveals three parts of the pattern.  First, theorem identity imposes
a common frame: human and AI proof sizes are strongly associated.  Second, AI provenance tracks a
change inside that frame: AI proofs contain more local and elaborated structure per named interface
element.  Third, this interior expansion neither produces a detectable reduction in modeled theorem
belief nor clearly predicts the small human--AI belief difference.  Architecture moves; the modeled
endpoint does not move with it.

This result advances the phase-transition account in a controlled setting.  Viteri and DeDeo
showed how a network of mutually supporting claims can sustain confidence despite fallible local
inference.  Their historical and Coq examples differed in theorem, scale, representation, and
provenance.  Here the theorem is fixed within every comparison.  The question is no longer only
whether one proof network can sustain belief, but whether that belief is robust to another
realization of the same formal target.

The paper proceeds in that order.  We first define epistemic decoupling and the interface--interior
distinction.  We then observe one paired comparison at two resolutions: the source script and the
elaborated proof term.  The results follow a single sequence---common constraint, interior change,
epistemic invariance.  We then consider what the pattern means for cognition, philosophy of proof,
and AI proof design.  \Cref{app:coqlean} returns to the original Coq analysis and asks directly what
the human Lean evidence confirms.

\section{Epistemic decoupling}

\subsection{From one proof to a paired contrast}

Let $S$ be a formal statement and let $P_H$ and $P_A$ be checked proof terms of $S$ with human and AI
provenance.  From a term $P$ we extract a directed acyclic expression graph $G(P)$.  Constant nodes
refer to named declarations in the ambient library.  We call this named frontier the proof's
\emph{interface}.  Applications, abstractions, variables, types, and other non-constant expressions
form its \emph{interior}.

The distinction is operational, not metaphysical.  A library constant is not necessarily a deep
mathematical idea, and a non-constant node is not necessarily a trivial implementation detail.  The
boundary asks a narrower question: how much internal expression structure is placed behind each
named point of contact with the surrounding library?  We measure that load by
\begin{equation}
  \chi(P)=\frac{|V(G(P))|-|C_V(P)|}{|C_D(P)|+1},
  \label{eq:interior}
\end{equation}
where $C_V(P)$ is the set of constant vertices in the structurally deduplicated graph and $C_D(P)$
is the set of distinct library declarations they name.  At source level, explicit local \code{have}
claims per tactic provide an independent, visible proxy for the same idea.

Let $B_\epsilon(P)$ be theorem-node belief under a network model with local error rate $\epsilon$.
For each theorem pair, define
\begin{equation}
  A=\log\frac{\chi(P_A)}{\chi(P_H)}, \qquad
  E=B_\epsilon(P_A)-B_\epsilon(P_H).
  \label{eq:decoupling}
\end{equation}
The empirical signature of decoupling is a systematic architectural contrast $A$ together with a
small epistemic contrast $E$ and little association between the two.  This definition makes the
claim falsifiable.  It would fail if larger AI interiors reliably produced lower theorem belief, or
if the structural contrast vanished under elaboration.

\subsection{Why a network can decouple size from belief}

A linear chain gives one intuition about proof reliability.  If every link can fail independently,
each additional step is another liability.  A network gives another.  Claims can be reused;
independent routes can converge; and a conclusion can provide abductive support for premises that
also support it.  Under the Viteri--DeDeo dynamics, path proliferation can offset the loss of
influence along any single path.  A sufficiently connected proof can therefore cross from local
uncertainty to high global confidence \citep{viteri2022}.

This mechanism makes decoupling possible but does not guarantee it.  Adding nodes could create
redundant support, extend fragile chains, or merely expose compiler bookkeeping.  The paired design
asks which of these possibilities dominates for current human- and AI-provenance Lean proofs.  It
also exposes a deeper issue: kernel expressions need not be the units on which a human reader
reasons.  If term-level expansion leaves network belief nearly unchanged, the cognitively consequential
variation may lie in how those expressions are grouped and presented.

\section{One comparison at two resolutions}

\subsection{The paired corpus}

The source data come from NuminaMath-LEAN proof artifacts \citep{numinamathlean}.  Each retained
record supplies one formal statement, one human-provenance proof, one AI-provenance proof, validation
metadata, and a coarse source label.  Both proofs must be marked valid and available, contain a
measurable proof body, and expose at least one premise-like reference under the corrected lexical
parser.  The source sample contains \SourcePairs{} theorem pairs from \SourceGroups{} source groups.

``Human'' and ``AI'' refer to dataset provenance, not pure causal treatments.  Human editing,
retrieval, shared training data, and selection can blur the categories.  The value of the design is
more basic: within each row, statement, hypotheses, and ambient formal environment are shared.  The
comparison therefore removes theorem identity, a major confound in cross-corpus studies.

We observe the same conceptual contrast at two resolutions.  \Cref{tab:measurement} gives the map.
The source and term samples are filtered separately, so evidence should be combined conceptually,
not by pretending that every estimate comes from one common table.

\subsection{Visible organization in source}

For each script we count lines, tactic invocations, and explicit \code{have} declarations.  We also
use two deliberately conservative lexical measures of named premise contact: a loose count of
distinct dotted or capitalized identifiers and a strict count of dotted identifiers.  Comments and
strings are removed first.  These counts are not dependency graphs.  Their role is to check whether
an apparent interface difference is visible before elaboration and whether it survives a change in
token rule.

To retain proofs with no explicit local claims, the source interior proxy is
\begin{equation}
  \chi_R=\frac{n_{\code{have}}+1/2}{n_{\mathrm{tactic}}+1}.
\end{equation}
Because this ratio uses pseudocounts, raw medians and paired differences are reported alongside it.

\subsection{Certificate structure after elaboration}

Lean elaboration turns each script into a kernel-checkable term.  We deduplicate structurally equal
expressions to form a DAG and direct edges from child expressions toward the expressions that
contain them.  The graph includes the theorem type and proof value.  Named constants remain at the
frontier; imported declarations are not recursively expanded.  This \code{term0} representation is
therefore the root certificate behind a fixed interface, not all of Mathlib.

Both sides were extracted for \EligibleTermPairs{} eligible pairs.  We exclude a pair if either graph
contains \code{sorryAx} or \code{SyntheticOpaque}, which mark Lean error recovery rather than a
completed proof.  The clean term sample contains \TermPairs{} pairs.  This 36.8\% pairwise exclusion
is substantial, and the retained terms should not be treated as a random sample of all valid source
proofs.  The source and term samples overlap on \CrossLayerN{} statements.

\subsection{The epistemic endpoint}

For each clean term graph we implement the symmetric-error case of the asymmetric-Ising model in
\citet{viteri2022}.  Nodes begin true with prior probability $0.75$.  Deductive and abductive
couplings correspond to local error rates $\epsilon\in\{.10,.05,.01\}$.  We run ten sweeps and 32
Monte Carlo chains per graph, using common seeds within each theorem pair.  Every retained graph is
one weak component containing the theorem root, so disconnected isolates do not affect this
comparison.

The primary endpoint is theorem-node belief averaged across the three error settings.  It is a
model implication, not a measurement of a reader.  This distinction matters throughout: the study
can establish decoupling inside the network model and use that result to motivate, but not replace,
a cognitive experiment.

\subsection{Paired uncertainty}

We report medians of paired differences or paired log ratios.  Spearman correlation measures how
strongly theorem identity constrains proof scale and how the two paired contrasts co-vary.  The
corpus contains many related competition problems, so ordinary proof-level intervals would be too
narrow.  Our intervals resample the \SourceGroups{} coarse problem-source groups with replacement,
retaining all observations in each selected group.  The analysis uses 20,000 resamples with a fixed
seed.  An identical ORCHARD run reproduced the local numerical summary byte for byte.

\section{The decoupling pattern}

\subsection{First, the theorem supplies a common frame}

Human and AI proofs of the same statement are not independent constructions.  Their script line
counts correlate at $\rho_s=\LineRho$ (\CI{} \LineRhoCI{}), and their elaborated node counts correlate
at $\rho_s=\NodeRho$ (\CI{} \NodeRhoCI{}).  \Cref{fig:constraint} shows the relationship at both
resolutions.

This result is the control on which the rest of the argument depends.  The machine proof is not
arbitrary text set against an organized human object.  Both proofs answer to the same type,
hypotheses, and library.  Provenance appears as a deformation of theorem-specific structure,
not as a replacement for it.

\subsection{Second, AI provenance tracks an interior shift}

At source level, the deformation appears as local decomposition.  The median script grows from 23
to 26 lines, a paired change of $+2$ (\CI{} $[1,3]$).  The median number of explicit \code{have}
claims grows from 3 to 6.  Relative to tactic count, the median paired AI/human ratio is
\HaveRatio{} (\CI{} \HaveRatioCI{}).  Equal weighting of the 12 source groups gives a smaller raw
\code{have} contrast of $+0.5$ $[0,1.5]$; the pooled direction is therefore not uniform across
groups.

Named premise contact shows no corresponding rise with this internal decomposition.  The loose and
strict source proxies each have paired median difference zero.  Their zero-width bootstrap intervals reflect
discrete counts, many ties, and only 12 clusters; they are not equivalence tests.  The defensible
claim is comparative: a clear source-level increase in local claims is not accompanied by a clear
increase in the breadth of the named interface.

Elaborated terms show the same asymmetry without relying on source tokens.  AI terms contain a
median 4,561 nodes, compared with 3,816.5 for human terms; the paired difference is $+290$
$([20,529])$.  The number of distinct constants is nearly unchanged: 200.5 for human terms and 199
for AI terms, with paired difference $-1$ $([-3,0])$.  AI terms therefore contain
\InteriorRatio{} times as many non-constant nodes per distinct constant (\CI{}
\InteriorRatioCI{}).  Giving every source group equal weight yields a similar ratio of 1.11
$[1.02,1.21]$.

The two interior measures are not identical, but they are not unrelated.  Among the
\CrossLayerN{} overlapping pairs, source-level change in local claims per tactic correlates with
term-level change in internal nodes per constant at $\rho_s=\CrossLayerRho$ (\CI{}
\CrossLayerRhoCI{}; \cref{fig:interior}).  The association is modest, as one would expect when a
tactic can elaborate into many expressions.  Its importance is methodological: the interior result
does not depend entirely on either source syntax or kernel structure.

Taken together, these measurements support one claim.  In this corpus, AI proofs often put more internal
scaffolding behind a named interface whose breadth changes much less.  The data do not identify the
mechanism.  Explicit subgoal decomposition, coercions, normalization, repeated tactic templates,
and less economical theorem APIs are all compatible with the result.  Nor does ``more interior''
mean ``less understanding.''  It names an architectural contrast, not a mental state.

\subsection{Third, the epistemic endpoint remains stable}

If each additional expression were an independent chance to fail, the larger AI interior would
reduce theorem confidence.  The network model does not produce that result.  Human and AI belief
profiles nearly overlap at all three error rates (\cref{fig:belief}).  Averaged across settings, the
mean paired AI-minus-human theorem-belief difference is \BeliefDifference{} (\CI{}
\BeliefDifferenceCI{}); the paired median is zero.  Every setting-specific cluster interval includes
zero.

More importantly, structural change offers little predictive information about epistemic change.
Across the \TermPairs{} pairs, the association between the AI/human log ratio in interior load and the paired change in mean
theorem belief is $\rho_s=\DecouplingRho$ (\CI{} \DecouplingRhoCI{}).  Within the precision of this
sample, proofs with much larger AI interiors show no clear tendency to receive lower modeled theorem
belief.

This conjunction is epistemic decoupling.  The architecture moves in a consistent direction, but
the modeled endpoint does not move with it.  The conclusion is stronger than saying that group
means happen to be close: we detect no tendency for proof pairs with the largest structural changes
to show a correspondingly larger epistemic contrast.  It is also narrower than a claim about human belief:
the invariance belongs to this network model at this representation.

\section{What decoupling means}

\subsection{The missing scale is the reader's scale}

Experts do not normally inspect every primitive expression with equal attention.  They move
backward from goals, examine warrants selectively, reconstruct omitted steps, test examples, and
treat familiar lemmas as chunks \citep{weber2008,inglis2012,mejiaramos2014,panse2018}.  The kernel
graph and the reader's proof are therefore different objects.

Epistemic decoupling makes that difference visible.  A certificate can grow internally while a
uniform network dynamics assigns its theorem nearly the same confidence.  If readers nevertheless
find one presentation clearer, that difference must depend on features absent from the model: attention,
chunking, names, prior familiarity, explanatory expectations, or navigation across levels.  The
interface is a plausible place to look because it supplies the handles by which a reader can compress
and revisit the interior.

This interpretation extends rather than rejects the phase-transition framework.  The original
model studies how evidence propagates through a fixed network.  A cognitive successor should study
how a reader constructs the effective network in the first place.  Node granularity, prior belief,
and attention should become endogenous to the interaction between proof and reader.

\subsection{Confidence cannot select the explanatory proof}

A formal certificate establishes derivability conditional on its statement and environment.  It
does not by itself establish that the formal statement captures the intended mathematics, or that
the certificate tracks the reasons a mathematician takes to matter
\citep{dedeo2026correspondence}.  Proofs can reveal methods, dependencies, and unifying ideas beyond
their conclusions \citep{rav1999,mancosu2008}.

The paired data add a selection problem.  Even after the statement is fixed, multiple checked
interiors remain possible.  The belief functional studied here supplies no clear criterion for
choosing among them.  Other
virtues must enter: explanatory relevance, modularity at a reader-appropriate scale, stability under
library change, and capacity for communal correction.  This is consistent with accounts of
mathematics in which reliability is secured not only by avoiding error, but by organizing work so
that errors can be localized and repaired \citep{avigad2020reliability,detoffoli2021}.

\subsection{Proof generation becomes proof design}

AI proof systems are commonly trained and evaluated first on whether they produce a certificate
that passes the kernel \citep{yang2023,ren2025}.  That objective has enabled remarkable progress,
but it is indifferent
among many proofs of the same theorem.  The
paired comparison shows that the indifference already leaves an architectural trace: AI proofs
often enlarge the interior without broadening the named interface.

The engineering response is not indiscriminate compression.  A longer proof may expose a useful
lemma; a shorter one may hide its main work inside automation.  The objective should be proof
design: choosing intermediate claims, names, dependencies, and abstraction boundaries so that a
valid term becomes a useful mathematical artifact.  Proof-refactoring systems already target
readability, modularity, maintenance, and reuse \citep{fu2026refactor}; studies of human--AI
formalization likewise point to the value of high-level control \citep{collins2026workflows}.
Epistemic decoupling explains why these goals cannot be reduced to correctness or modeled confidence.

\section{Tests that would break the decoupling claim}

The present result is model-internal.  A decisive next study should hold correctness and theorem
fixed while intervening on architecture.  For 40--60 Lean theorems, construct four checked
presentations: the human-provenance proof, a raw AI proof, a refactored AI proof with designed
intermediate lemmas, and a mechanically expanded human proof that preserves the original plan.
Randomize the displayed authorship label independently of the underlying proof.

Readers should perform a small set of preregistered tasks: validate the argument, locate a seeded
defect in an informal rendering, summarize the main idea, identify essential assumptions, transfer
the method to a nearby theorem, and repair the proof after an API change.  The primary outcomes are
validation accuracy, calibrated confidence, conceptual recall, transfer, and repair cost.  Process
measures---time, navigation traces, and inspected interface elements---can test the mechanism.

This design can falsify the present interpretation in several ways.  Interior expansion may lower
human confidence even when the network model is invariant.  Refactoring may fail to improve
comprehension.  Authorship labels may dominate architecture.  Or the apparent interface may prove
to be the wrong unit once reader behavior is observed.  Each failure would locate the scale at which
the current model stops being adequate.

A parallel library study should follow accepted human-, AI-, and mixed-provenance contributions
through review and later use.  Outcomes include citation by later declarations, survival through API
changes, repair time, reviewer effort, and reuse of introduced lemmas.  This would test the social
half of the thesis: whether an intelligently designed interface helps a certificate become communal
mathematical infrastructure \citep{pantsar2026}.

\section{Limitations}

The pairing removes theorem identity as a confound, but it does not identify a pure causal effect of
AI authorship.  Provenance labels may conceal editing, retrieval, shared data, and selection.  The
corpus is dominated by competition mathematics, and its 12 source groups are coarse.

The two interior measures are proposed constructs.  Source identifiers are lexical heuristics.
Term constants are more authoritative, but the graph boundary still depends on Lean, Mathlib, and
the elaborator version.  Pairwise removal of error-recovery terms excludes 36.8\% of eligible pairs
and may select on proof complexity.  The cross-layer association is positive but modest.

The belief result is not behavioral evidence.  Thirty-two chains reduce Monte Carlo noise but do
not validate the prior, symmetric error rates, node granularity, or update rule as a model of human
reading.  The near-zero contrast should be read diagnostically: within this representation and
model, a larger AI interior is not accompanied by a detectable epistemic deficit.

Finally, decoupling is not equivalence.  Several intervals include zero because the data do not
resolve a contrast; they do not prove identity.  Conversely, a statistically detectable structural
effect need not be cognitively important.  The reader experiments above are required to establish
that bridge.

\section{Conclusion}

Holding the theorem fixed changes what can be asked of a proof.  Human and AI constructions share a
strong theorem-imposed frame, but they distribute work differently within it.  AI-provenance proofs
in this corpus place more visible and elaborated structure behind each named interface element.  The network
dynamics that motivated epistemic phase transitions nevertheless assign the two populations nearly
the same theorem belief, and the size of the interior change has little association with the belief
contrast.

That is epistemic decoupling: architecture changes while a specified epistemic endpoint remains
stable.  Its importance lies as much in what it withholds as in what it establishes.  Correctness and
modeled confidence do not select the proof that a reader can understand, a maintainer can repair, or
a library can reuse.  In a world of abundant certificates, the scientific object is the coupled
system of theorem, proof, interface, reader, and community.

\appendix
\section{From the original Coq networks to human Lean proofs}
\label{app:coqlean}

This appendix asks a direct question: do the human Lean networks confirm the principal structural
and epistemic findings reported by \citet{viteri2022}?  The answer is yes for the conceptual core,
but not for every distributional claim.

\subsection{Making the comparison commensurable}

The original study extracted abstract-syntax trees from Coq proofs, identified repeated claims, and
converted the trees into DAGs.  The updated human Lean sample applies the analogous pipeline to 33
classic and frontier theorems in Mathlib: proof terms are expanded level by level, structurally
identical expressions are shared, and expansion stops after crossing roughly 10,000 nodes.  These
expanded term graphs are the appropriate Lean comparison.  Declaration graphs, which connect named
theorems across a library, have a different boundary and are reported only as a representation
sensitivity check.

The historical count requires care.  The published article describes 48 Coq proofs.  Its archived
table supplies \PublishedCoqN{} matchable exponent entries, while the recovered directories yield
\RecoveredCoqN{} analyzable graphs under the updated pipeline.  The archive also contains a status
list, but it includes aliases and does not specify a unique 48-graph analysis set.  We therefore
report the actual sample size for each check rather than forcing the archive into one total.

\subsection{The fixed tail at \texorpdfstring{$x_{\min}=10$}{xmin = 10}}

The main exponent discrepancy arose from a cutoff choice.  The surviving publication script fixed
$x_{\min}=10$; a later audit initially allowed software to choose $x_{\min}$ by minimizing a
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance.  Those procedures estimate different parts of the degree distribution.
When the fixed-10 convention is restored, 40 of the 44 estimable archived exponents agree with the
published values within 0.01, and 43 agree within 0.02; the median absolute discrepancy is 0.003.
This is direct provenance rather than a reconstruction from the table alone: the archived fitter
hard-codes \texttt{cutf=10}, and saved plotting data record that cutoff together with the published
Four Color and G\"odel exponents.

The convention was not completely uniform.  The four smallest published networks do not reproduce
under the surviving fixed-10 call.  Applying the same discrete fitter at $x_{\min}=5$ recovers both
their exponents and standard errors after rounding.  No surviving script contains this fixed-5
branch, so the evidence supports a small-network exception in an earlier or uncommitted version,
but does not recover its rule.  One further row, Triangle Inequality, differs by 0.012 at
$x_{\min}=10$.  These historical exceptions do not enter the cross-assistant comparison below,
which applies one declared cutoff uniformly to every estimable Coq and Lean network.

For the tail that begins at reuse degree 10, recovered Coq networks have median
$\alpha=\CoqAlpha$ (network-bootstrap interval \CoqAlphaCI{}; $n=45$).  The \HumanLeanN{} human Lean
term networks have median $\alpha=\LeanAlpha$ (\LeanAlphaCI{}).  The median difference is about
$-0.05$, and a two-sided Mann--Whitney comparison gives $p=0.124$.  Thus a fixed-tail index close to
two recurs across the two samples.  This is a qualitative replication, not an equivalence test; it
answers the explicitly restricted question of what happens above reuse degree 10.

Fixing the cutoff does not, by itself, establish a unique distribution family.  We therefore refit
both alternatives on exactly the tail $k\geq10$.  Four recovered Coq networks have fewer than ten
observations in that tail and are not estimable.  Against an exponential, the power law is favored
in 43 of the remaining 45 Coq networks and is inconclusive in two; it is favored in all 33 human
Lean networks.  Against a lognormal, the power law is favored in neither collection.  The lognormal
is favored in two Coq networks and the remaining 43 are inconclusive; all 33 Lean comparisons are
inconclusive.  Thus reuse above degree 10 is heavy relative to an exponential, but these data
usually cannot distinguish a power law from a lognormal.  We do not confirm a universal,
cutoff-free pure power law.

This out-degree result is also sensitive to representation, although not in the same way as local
arity.  We repeated the fixed-$x_{\min}=10$ analysis after putting Coq and Lean into the common
binary root-proof schema described below.  Only \NormOutCoqFits{} of \NormOutCoqN{} Coq roots and
\NormOutLeanFits{} of \NormOutLeanN{} human Lean roots then had enough tail observations and
variation to fit.  Their median exponents remain in the same neighborhood
($\alpha=\NormOutCoqAlpha$ and $\NormOutLeanAlpha$), but a power law beats an exponential in only
3/23 and 1/12 fits; it loses in 4/23 and 1/12, with the rest inconclusive.  It never beats a
lognormal.  Thus the exponent near two is recurrent where a root-proof tail exists, but the strong
expanded-graph evidence against an exponential does not survive the change of proof boundary.
Constructor normalization and removal of recursive library expansion occur together here, so this
check cannot assign the change to one of them alone.

The same-theorem result is correspondingly modest.  Among \NormOutPairN{} pairs for which both
normalized roots are estimable, median $\alpha$ is \NormOutHumanAlpha{} for human proofs and
\NormOutAIAlpha{} for AI proofs.  The paired shift is only \NormOutPairDiff{} ($p=\NormOutPairP$),
despite its statistical detectability.  Proof identity again constrains the two populations more
clearly than tail family distinguishes them.

\subsection{Does local arity follow a Poisson distribution?}
\label{app:poisson}

This claim cannot be compared across assistants until the objects being counted are made the same.
With edges directed from premise to dependent, in-degree counts the immediate children of an AST
node: its local arity.  But local arity is partly a choice made by the language and extractor.  We
therefore separate a historical replication in the archived representation from a new comparison
in a common representation.

\paragraph{Historical Coq representation.}
The archived \code{histogram\_in.rb} removed degree-zero leaves and overlaid a Poisson using the
mean positive degree.  Conditional on $K>0$, however, the maximum-likelihood parameter solves
\[
  \bar k=\frac{\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda}}.
\]
We refit this zero-truncated Poisson to each of the 49 recovered Coq graphs.  Absolute fit is tested
by a discrete Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistic with 199 parametric bootstrap samples, refitting the
parameter in every sample; rejections use Benjamini--Hochberg correction.  All 49 graphs reject.
By AIC, a Conway--Maxwell--Poisson (CMP) model \citep{shmueli2005} wins 35 comparisons and a
negative binomial wins 14.  Restoring leaves does not rescue the model: a zero-inflated Poisson
(ZIP) is rejected in all 49 and is never the best full-distribution model; hurdle CMP wins 35 and
hurdle negative binomial wins 14.  This is a direct correction to the historical visual fit, but it
is a result about the native archived Coq graphs.

\paragraph{Why the native Lean result is almost always two.}
Lean represents an application as \code{Expr.app f a}, a binary node.  The archived Coq
\code{CoqAST} instead stores \code{App} with a head and a list of arguments, and its graph converter
joins all of them to one node; it also retains binder names as children.  In the pooled native
graphs, applications have mean arity 4.013 in Coq and exactly 2 in Lean.  Applications account for
59.0\% and 84.7\% of positive-arity nodes, respectively.  This explains the earlier result: 96.7\%
of native human-Lean positive nodes have degree two, against 10.9\% in native Coq.  The contrast is
primarily representational.  We therefore withdraw the direct native-format Coq--Lean comparison.

\paragraph{Common-schema reanalysis.}
We normalize both corpora to the root proof value, excluding the theorem type and recursive library
expansion.  Applications are binary in both; binder names are removed while binder domain and body
are retained; and \code{let} keeps its type, value, and body.  The primary positive sample contains
only the constructors shared across systems---application, lambda, product, and let.  Structural
hash-consing then produces a DAG in each system.  A sensitivity analysis retaining system-specific
constructors gives the same conclusion.  Networks with fewer than 20 positive shared-core nodes
are excluded, leaving \NormCoqN{} of 49 Coq proofs and \NormLeanN{} of 33 human Lean proofs; all 312
matched human--AI pairs remain eligible.

The same-theorem human--AI comparison is similarly uninformative about authorship: its median
degree-two shares are \NormHumanDegreeTwo{} and \NormAIDegreeTwo{}.  Both populations pass through
the same elaborator and constructor grammar.  The defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than
the original claim.  Local dependence is small and concentrated.  Reuse is broad and heavy-tailed
at the expanded-network scale, but its tail evidence weakens at the root-proof boundary; local
arity itself is representation-dependent, and a Poisson law is not confirmed.  This does not
weaken the main epistemic-decoupling result, whose paired structural and belief measurements do not
rely on a universal degree distribution.

\subsection{What confirms---and what AI adds}

Modularity is especially stable at the term level: median $Q$ is \CoqModularity{} in the recovered
Coq graphs and \LeanModularity{} in the human Lean graphs.  At $\epsilon=.01$, both collections reach
median theorem belief 1.0; the median belief averaged over all nodes is \CoqMeanBelief{} and
\LeanMeanBelief{}, respectively.  Every recovered Coq and human Lean term graph also has a positive
firewall contrast under the updated implementation.  The sign agrees with the original claim that
modules can localize reversals, but its magnitude should not be compared to the published
$\Delta\mathcal L_1$: the normalization changed.

bounded local arity and broader reuse is visible, but neither a Poisson local law nor one
representation-invariant reuse-tail family is confirmed.

The representation check explains why these confirmations must remain qualified.  For the same 33
Lean theorems, declaration graphs have median $Q=0.465$ and fixed-tail median $\alpha=1.751$, far from
the term-graph values.  A proof network has no representation-free exponent or modularity.  The
cross-language agreement in the expanded graphs is meaningful as a replication at a similar
analytic scale, not as evidence for a privileged or representation-free atomic scale.

The overall verdict is therefore precise.  Human Lean terms confirm that fine-grained expanded
proof graphs can exhibit concentrated reuse, strong modularity, positive firewall structure, and
high theorem belief under bidirectional dynamics.  The paired AI extension adds near-identical
root-level reuse indices and theorem belief, as well as the same grammar-constrained local arity;
paired modularity and firewall comparisons remain open.  None of these results justifies the
stronger claim that all proof networks instantiate one pure power law with a universal exponent,
or that network summaries are unchanged when the graph boundary moves to the root proof value.
Epistemic decoupling depends on the paired measurements reported in the main text, not on exact
scale freedom.
