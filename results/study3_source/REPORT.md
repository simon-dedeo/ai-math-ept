# Study 3 — Machine-generated vs human-written Lean 4 source (textual/structural)

Corpora compared (no Lean compilation; pure source analysis):

- **HUMAN_sp** — human team's `Sphere-Packing-Lean` at the last commit before 2026-02-15:
  `e075be66` (2026-02-02). 89 `.lean` files, 22,563 raw lines; 16,182 non-blank lines inside declarations.
- **GAUSS_sp** — the Gauss layer of Math Inc's fork (`corpora/mathinc-sphere-packing`, single squashed
  commit "Add dim 8 and dim 24 formalizations", 2026-03-02; 830 files, 180,661 lines): all declarations
  whose (fully-qualified) name does **not** occur in the HUMAN_sp snapshot. 6,194 declarations, 115,450 lines.
- **GAUSS_pnt** — Math Inc's `strongpnt` repo (strong PNT), entirely Gauss-generated: 8 files,
  28,407 lines; 1,124 declarations, 21,508 declaration lines.

## Authorship attribution method and caveats

- The human repo has full history (1,090 commits, 2024-02-28 → 2026-07-30). Both Math Inc repos are
  **squashed to a single commit**, so line-level `git blame` attribution is impossible there; we used the
  **diff between the pre-2026-02-15 human snapshot and the Math Inc tree**.
- PR #341 on the human repo ("Gauss: complete formalization of E₈ optimality in ℝ⁸", opened 2026-02-23 by
  `augustepoiroux`, **still open** as of 2026-07-31): **+52,187 / −15,457 lines, 287 files, 18 commits**.
  The PR body states the branch "was generated by Gauss (Math Inc's autoformalization agent)". The
  `mathinc-sphere-packing` corpus is a superset of that PR (it adds the dimension-24 formalization).
- Declaration-level attribution in the Math Inc tree: of 7,474 declarations, **100 are verbatim** (whitespace-
  normalized) matches of human-snapshot declarations, **1,180 share a name** with a human declaration but have
  altered text (module-system migration + Gauss's "project-wide proof golf" touched nearly everything), and
  **6,194 have new names** → these form the GAUSS_sp layer. File-level: 795 of 830 files (171,060 lines) are
  at paths that did not exist in the human snapshot; 35 files are modified versions of human files; 0 identical.
- Caveats: (i) human declarations *renamed* by Gauss would be misclassified as Gauss (direction: inflates the
  Gauss layer slightly with human-styled code, i.e. biases *against* finding differences); (ii) the excluded
  "modified" declarations are human-origin/Gauss-golfed hybrids and belong to neither layer cleanly;
  (iii) the human repo's own history includes some tool-assisted commits (e.g. "[codex] …" commit titles appear
  after the snapshot date; pre-snapshot commits are overwhelmingly attributed to named team members);
  (iv) GAUSS_sp includes the Dim24/Leech-lattice + coding-theory material, mathematically different terrain
  from the human layer; GAUSS_pnt (analytic number theory) is the cleaner like-for-like second sample;
  (v) all parsing is regex-based (declarations, proof bodies after the first top-level `:=`, comment-stripped),
  and the citation graph matches names by word-boundary token (bare names must not be dot-prefixed; dotted
  names also match fully qualified) — name shadowing with Mathlib can still cause residual false positives.

*definitions = def+abbrev+structure+class+inductive; theorems = theorem+lemma.

The "AI adds lemmas but not definitions" hypothesis holds **strongly for GAUSS_pnt** (0.04 — a 28k-line
development introducing only 44 definitional objects) but **not for GAUSS_sp** (0.27 > human 0.19), where the
Dim24 coding-theory/lattice content forced many small combinatorial `def`s (`BinWord`, `Code`, `Block`, …).
What is uniform across both Gauss samples is the shift from `theorem` to `lemma` (87–93% of proved statements
are `lemma` vs 58% for humans).

Per opportunity-to-be-cited, a GAUSS_sp lemma is reused **~3.4× less** than a human lemma (0.38 vs 1.31 per

(Caveat: the size-matched control also penalizes sheer project size — locality of reference means a lemma in
a 180k-line tree naturally serves a smaller fraction of it; raw and matched views bracket the truth.)

## 4. Near-duplicate proof text

5-line shingles of whitespace-normalized proof lines; duplication rate = share of shingle instances whose
shingle occurs more than once in the layer.

| | HUMAN_sp | GAUSS_sp | GAUSS_pnt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shingle instances | 9,268 | 84,172 | 14,541 |
| 5-line shingle duplication rate | 7.9% | 5.9% | 3.4% |
| `have … := …` lines | 1,458 | 17,777 | 4,609 |
| duplicated identical `have` lines | 20.1% | **29.4%** | 12.9% |
| most repeated `have` (count) | `have hz := ne_zero z` (7) | `have ht0 : 0 < t := ht.1` (36), `have hzq : q (z:ℂ) ≠ 0 := q_ne_zero (z:ℂ)` (26) | `have Z :=` (10) |

The blanket claim "Gauss repeats local patterns rather than factoring reusable lemmas" is **only partially
supported at the surface-text level**: multi-line block duplication is actually *lower* in the Gauss layers
(5.9% / 3.4% vs 7.9%), and the human rate is not an artifact — the Gauss shingle base is 9× larger, giving
*more* collision opportunity, which makes its lower rate conservative. The repetition Gauss does exhibit is
at the *single-step* level: 29% of its `have` lines in sphere-packing are verbatim repeats (vs 20% human),
consistent with re-deriving the same local facts (`0 < t`, `q z ≠ 0`) dozens of times instead of citing a
shared lemma — which is also what the reuse graph in §3 shows.

## Interpretation

Gauss's Lean source is structurally distinctive on every axis measured. It writes in `lemma`-sized bricks
(87–93% of proved statements, vs 58% human), with proofs twice as long and four times as many explicit `have`
steps, re-proving small local facts verbatim (29% duplicated `have` lines) rather than promoting them into
shared library lemmas: per opportunity, its sphere-packing declarations are cited ~3.4× less than human ones,
and in a size-matched comparison two-thirds are never reused at all. Its Mathlib usage is equally dense but
~40% less diverse, i.e. it leans hard on a familiar core API. The "no new abstractions" hypothesis needs
refinement: when the mathematics forces definitional infrastructure (Dim24 codes/lattices) Gauss does mint
definitions (def:thm 0.27), but in theorem-proving terrain (strong PNT: 0.04) it produces almost pure lemma
chains. Against all this stands the headline capability result: the agent delivered a kernel-checked,
sorry-free E₈ optimality proof (+52k lines, PR #341) in ~3 weeks, on a problem where the human team had spent
two years and still carried ~92 sorries. The trade is legible: velocity and completeness at the cost of
architecture — code that proves, but does not organize.
